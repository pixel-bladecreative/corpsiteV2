The Monk's rule is hands empty and open, always. The shadow form carries a sword. Empty hands are the graduation — the student needs a blade to show what he might be; the master no longer needs one to be it.

If he is meant as a separate character rather than an earlier form, the file splits cleanly. None of the mechanics depend on the reading.

Finding one

He has no colour at all.

I expected his ink to be petrol — the way the Monk's cloth turned out to be bronze. It isn't. Sampled across all three plates it returns #171714 · #1B1B18 · #1C1C19 — hue 60, saturation 11–13%. Warm-neutral sumi black. No brand hue whatsoever.

Which is a better answer than the one I guessed. The final form is the bronze family desaturated — he is made of the brand. The shadow form is made of nothing. He has not earned a hue yet. He is colourless because he has not yet become anything.

That now stands as a rule for the whole cast: a figure's hue states where it sits on the arc, and colour is earned.

Finding two

The blade belongs to no palette.

Sampled at hue 174–177, saturation 76–82%, value 81–86%. Petrol is hue 197 — this sits 22° warmer and far hotter. It is not petrol, not bronze, and in neither the day nor the night palette.

It is the brightest, most saturated value anywhere in this system, and it appears in exactly one place. Which is correct, not a problem: it is the only colour in the brand that means potential rather than a material or a mode — precisely what you asked it to carry.
