The correction

Photograph, then brush stroke.

Where he is solid he must be a photograph — every straw fibre and split in the hat, the coarse broken weave of the outer robe, the real sheen and drape of the silk, individually grained beads, weathered skin with sharp tendons. The same material standard as the Monk. Nothing illustrated.

The dissolution is then the only stylised element in the frame — and that is exactly what makes it read as loss.

The face

Always shadowed. Total black beneath the brim, no features at any angle. Inside that darkness, one small dim amber coal — the only warm light on him, and a light source rather than a hue, the same rule as the lantern colour. It is the one thing he carries of what he will become.

The blade

Kindle belongs to no palette.

Sampled at hue 174–177, saturation 76–82%, value 81–86%. Petrol is hue 197 — this sits 22° warmer and far hotter. It is not petrol, not bronze, and in neither the day nor the night palette.

It is the brightest, most saturated value anywhere in this system, and it appears in exactly one place. Which is correct, not a problem: it is the only colour in the brand that means potential rather than a material or a mode — precisely what you asked it to carry.

Kindle

Absolute rules. Image gamut only — 1.69:1 on paper, so it can never be type, a tile, or a border. Only ever behind a blade. It never touches the figure carrying it. Kindle anywhere else in the system is a bug.

Mechanics

Half of him stays unfinished.

  • Always the hat

    A wide conical kasa, and beneath it total black. No features, ever, at any angle — plus one dim amber coal.

  • Hyper-real solids

    Every straw fibre in the hat, the broken weave of the outer robe, the sheen and drape of the silk, grained beads, weathered skin. Total material fidelity — the same standard as the Monk.

  • Ragged transition

    Where photograph becomes ink the boundary is uneven — abrupt in places, gradual in others. Never a clean line, never a uniform fade.

  • The weapon holds colour

    Kindle bleeds behind the blade only. The figure stays black and grey.

  • Dance, not violence

    Sweeping and unhurried even mid-cut.

  • Do not resolve him

    The pull under generation is toward a clean, complete, well-drawn character. Half of him must stay unfinished — that is the character, not a limitation of it.
