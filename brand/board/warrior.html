He contains both ends

On a dark ground his light layers carry him. On a light ground his dark layers do. No adaptation needed — which is exactly why your three references work equally on cream, dark brown and grey-blue.

Placement decides the mode. The figure does not change; only the feather does.

The wardrobe

Never one garment.

The layering is the character. If the cloth reads as a single material, the figure has failed — the same layering logic as the tech diorama and the fold.

  • Bone linen under-robeFine, soft, close to the body.

  • Coarse hemp over-robeStiff and sculptural, hem torn into long ragged tongues that fan with the movement.

  • Rope sashWound many times at the waist. Dry, hard, dark.

  • Wrapped stripsFrayed cloth and cord at forearm and shin.

  • Ochre shoulder panelHanging loose, the one warm note in the cloth.

Rules

What holds for every figure.

  • Sharp figure

    Never motion-blurred. The motion lives in the ribbons, the dissolving particles and the streaming cloth — which is what lets him read as moving while standing still.

  • Dissolves at edges

    Hems, sleeves, the away side breaking into drifting particles and mist. Never fully contained by his own outline.

  • Face at rest

    Eyes down or closed, calm, no strain. The violence is in the body. The most fragile rule under generation — ask for "action" and you get a shouting face every time.

  • No weapon

    Hands empty and open, always.

  • Dance before strike

    The pose reads as choreography as much as combat. Even the crouch is coiled rather than braced.

  • Ribbons carry colour

    Cloth stays desaturated; petrol teal and bronze/rust enter through the brushwork. The only place brand saturation touches a figure.
