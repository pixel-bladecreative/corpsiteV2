Pixel Blade · round two · supersedes the JPS palette

Forge, garden, livery.

A palette is not a list of colors. It is a set of materials — each with a base value, a surface behavior, and a rule for how it answers the light. Round one defined colors, which was half a system.

The three materials

Graphite, bronze, petrol.

Every value below was sampled off the plates further down, not chosen. The multiple values per material are not tints to pick from freely — they are the same material at different angles to one hard raking light. Two light sources anywhere in the system is a bug.

Graphite

Matte powder-coat, fine tooth

Non-reflective. Reads across a wide range purely by angle. The body material — and it never carries text at any value.

#060706#1A1A1A#3F3937#817C7A

Bronze

Brushed satin, directional grain

Grain runs along each plane's long axis, one direction per plane. Catches light as a soft sheen, never a glossy point. 8.84:1 on the ground — it carries body text.

#9A7E67#E59E5D#EBA564

Petrol

Deep satin blue-green

A second ground, not an accent — bronze reads AAA on it. This is what keeps the palette off the law-firm shelf.

Plate · materials

Left to right: graphite, bronze, petrol. The bronze grain is the point — it is what separates machined from plated.

The signature

The hot edge is white, not gold.

That was the surprise on sampling. Where two planes meet, the specular highlight blows past the material color entirely and lands at #FEFEF5 — near-white. The bronze sits just inboard of it.

Getting this wrong — a thick gold line instead of a hairline white one — is precisely the difference between machined and plated. One pixel. Never a glow, never a blur, never thicker. The dividers inside the material ramps above are drawn this way.

Why this matters beyond decoration

A single hard raking light across faceted planes is the same lighting model as a folded paper crane. The mark and the environment are lit identically — which means the identity and the world it sits in stop being two separate problems.

Proof

The rule I got wrong.

Round one

"Gold is ink, not metal. Flat, one tone, no gloss, no bevel, no gradient, no metallic sheen. The moment gold looks like metal, this becomes a law firm."

Round two

Bronze is metal in the forge and ink in the livery. Brushed grain and hot edges on dimensional surfaces; dead flat for type, because text has to be flat to stay readable.

The law-firm failure was never metal. It is polished plated gold — mirror finish, bevels, a gradient standing in for a highlight. Brushed bronze under a raking light is machined and industrial. A different object entirely, and the plate on the left below is the disproof.

Blades are no longer steel. Pixels.

Open

What I need from you.

  • 01
    Bronze at #E59E5D — hot enough, or too hot?

    This is meaningfully more orange and more saturated than the round-one gold. It is closer to copper than to gold now. Say if it should pull back toward the tan.

  • 02
    Is petrol a ground or an accent?

    I have promoted it to a second ground — full panels and surfaces. That is a bigger move than treating it as a third color, and it changes how much of the site is not black.

  • 03
    Does the garden survive?

    The Kyoto night plates are warm amber against blue-grey. Petrol sits between them and reads as a bridge — but a machined material world and a photographic grown one is a lot of range. Worth deciding whether the site holds both or picks one.

  • 04
    Bronze crane, or drawn crane?

    The mark can be a photographed made object or a flat drawn one. Round one assumed drawn. The plate above argues otherwise. It cannot be both.
