A dark gallery interior, photographed straight on, symmetrical. A constructed exhibition space, not a real garden.

At the centre, presented as an exhibited object: a single ancient pine, heavily sculpted in flat horizontal layers, sitting on a low matte black plinth that appears to float clear of the floor. The tree is lit from one side and reads mostly as a dark mass with its needle layers catching a thin warm bronze rim.

Behind it, a tall panel of deep blue-green teal glass, glowing faintly from within, taller than the tree. The gallery walls are matte charcoal, broken only by thin bronze reveal lines running horizontally at the base and near the ceiling — hairline-thin, brilliant.

A single robed human figure stands below and in front of the plinth, seen from behind, rendered TINY at roughly four percent of the frame height.

The floor is polished dark stone, softly reflecting the teal panel and the bronze reveals.

Light: a narrow shaft falls from a slot in the ceiling directly onto the specimen. Everything else is in deep shadow. Roughly seventy-five percent of the frame is near-black.

Color: matte charcoal, deep blue-green teal glass, warm bronze reveals, near-white edge highlights. Nothing else — no coral, no red, no white walls, no green foliage saturation.

Style: constructed, museum-like, reverent, still, and slightly impossible. Calm but strange.
