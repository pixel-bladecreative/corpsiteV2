Anime key art in the style of Shinji Aramaki — hard-surface mechanical design, crisp panel lines, cel-shaded with flat colour zones and sharp specular edges.

THE MOMENT: the aftermath. A dim future-Tokyo interior — a service corridor of grey machine panelling, hard geometric plating, dead screens and cable runs. Something has already happened here and it is completely over. Fine dust hangs in the air. Nothing moves.

THE FIGURE, standing alone in the middle distance, small in the frame and facing away from the viewer: the young cyber-ninja in a dark hooded jacket and modified cargo trousers over matte black articulated plating. NO HAT — hood up, head slightly lowered, face entirely unseen. His posture is relaxed and unhurried, arms at his sides. One matte black cybernetic hand hangs open.

He is utterly still. The stillness is the threat.

TECH: narrow cyan-turquoise light channels along his spine plating and the prosthetic's knuckles — thin and hard-edged, the only saturated colour in the frame, and the only thing that reads as alive.

THE COMMON THREAD: from the waist down he is already coming apart — his legs and the hem of his jacket dissolving into WET BLACK INK and SMOKY SHADOW, curling tendrils drifting outward across the floor. A second, fainter vortex of the same ink hangs further down the corridor, as though he has recently arrived from there.

SETTING: hard grey machine panelling, deep shadow, one weak cold overhead light far above. Roughly seventy-five percent of the frame is near-black. No bodies visible, no gore, no damage shown — only emptiness and dust.

Color: near-black, cold machine grey, faint steel, and narrow cyan-turquoise light. Nothing else.

Style: Shinji Aramaki anime, cel-shaded, hard-surface. Quiet, cold, and ominous. NOT photorealistic, NOT painterly.

No text, no readable letters, no watermark, no logo, no visible face, no blood, no bodies.
