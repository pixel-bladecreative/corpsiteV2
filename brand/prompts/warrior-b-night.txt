A full-figure martial arts study on a deep near-black ground, lit from one side, with the figure emerging out of darkness.

THE FIGURE: a lean shaven-headed monk, a fictional character, coiled low in a crouch, both fists drawn up and close, weight sunk into bent legs, as though he has just landed and is about to move again. His head is tilted down, eyes closed, expression completely calm — no aggression, no strain. NO WEAPON; hands empty.

THE WARDROBE — many distinct layers, each a different weave and value, all worn and honestly aged:
  - a bone-white linen under-robe
  - a heavy coarse hemp over-robe in warm grey-tan, stiff and sculptural, hem torn into ragged tongues
  - a broad rope sash wound many times at the waist
  - forearms and shins bound in frayed cloth strips and cord
Each layer catches the single light differently — linen soft, hemp coarse and fibrous, rope dry. Real cloth with real weight.

LIGHT: one hard warm light from the upper left. The lit side of the figure glows warm bone and tan; the shadow side falls almost entirely to black. No fill light. Roughly seventy percent of the frame carries no detail at all.

DISSOLUTION: the outer cloth edges — torn hem, trailing sleeve, the far shoulder and the whole shadow side — break into fine drifting particles and pale dust that dissolve completely into the surrounding blackness. He is sharp and in focus where he is solid, never motion-blurred.

MOTION AS BRUSHWORK: a few sweeping calligraphic ink ribbons arc past him, drawn as wet brush strokes with visible bristle texture and tapered ends, catching the warm light — in warm bronze-orange and a single deep teal. Fine grit and spatter trails them.

Color: near-black, bone, warm grey-tan, rope brown, one warm amber light, with bronze-orange and deep teal in the brushwork only.

Style: dreamlike, cinematic, still and strange. Watercolour, mist and motion. NOT a photograph of a real person, NOT a film still, no modern clothing.

No text, no letters, no calligraphy characters, no seals, no watermark.
