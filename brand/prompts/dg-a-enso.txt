A vast dark stage set, photographed straight on. Not a real place — a constructed installation in a black void.

Filling the upper two thirds: an ENORMOUS perfect circle, many storeys tall, standing free with no visible support. The circle is a translucent screen lit from behind, glowing a soft warm bronze that is brightest at its centre and falls off toward its rim. Fine vertical striations run down it like brushed metal or raw silk. A hairline of brilliant near-white light catches its outer edge.

Standing in front of the disc, in complete silhouette: a single old pine, its trunk leaning, its branches spreading in flat horizontal layers. Pure black shape, no interior detail, drawn as though cut from paper.

At the base, on a low pale plinth, TWO HUMAN FIGURES seen from behind, rendered TINY — no more than three percent of the frame height. They are the only thing at human scale and they establish that the disc is impossible.

The floor is a seamless black mirror, perfectly still, doubling the disc, the tree and the figures into the bottom of the frame.

Everything outside the disc is deep void black, dissolving at the frame edges. Fine volumetric light falls from high above, made visible by haze.

Color: deep void black, warm bronze glow, one near-white edge, and a faint deep blue-green teal in the shadows. Nothing else — no coral, no red, no pink, no purple, no orange sunset.

Style: constructed, staged, still, and strange. Calm but impossible. High-end exhibition photography, not photojournalism.

No text, no letters, no calligraphy, no watermark, no logo, no signature.
