Extreme close product photograph of a sculptural wall relief made of large angular folded planes.

GEOMETRY — critical: the planes are derived from ORIGAMI FOLDS. Irregular, asymmetric, varied in size, meeting at hard straight creases and sharp vertices at unequal angles. Absolutely NO hexagons, NO honeycomb, NO regular tessellation, NO repeating grid, NO uniform cubes. The arrangement must look folded and hand-derived, not machine-tiled. Planes sit at clearly different depths, some pushed forward, some receding into shadow.

MATERIALS — exactly three, used as large flat areas:
1. Matte graphite. A deep near-black charcoal with a soft powder-coated finish, no reflection at all, reading in several values from almost pure black to a dark slate depending on its angle to the light.
2. Brushed bronze. Warm orange-gold metal with fine parallel BRUSHED GRAIN clearly visible running along each plane, satin rather than mirror. It catches the light as a soft directional sheen, never as a glossy highlight.
3. Satin petrol. A deep desaturated blue-green teal with a low satin finish, dark and moody, roughly the darkness of the graphite but unmistakably green-blue.

THE SIGNATURE DETAIL: where two planes meet at a crease, a razor-thin brilliant hot bronze line catches the light along the very edge. These edge highlights are hairline-thin, extremely bright, and are the sharpest thing in the image.

Light: a single hard raking light from the upper left. Planes facing it are fully lit; planes turned away fall to near-black. Strong directional contrast, no fill light, no ambient glow.

Color: matte charcoal black, warm brushed bronze, deep petrol teal. Nothing else. No purple, no blue, no chrome, no silver, no rainbow reflections.

Camera: straight on, shallow depth of field falling off at the edges, macro detail on the surface grain. Photographic realism, high-end product photography.
