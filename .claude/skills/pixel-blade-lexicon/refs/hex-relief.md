# ref: hex-relief

**Tags:** `material`
**Source:** supplied 2026-08-25. Not stored on disk.

Vertical image. A relief wall of extruded hexagonal and cube forms in isometric
arrangement, at varied depths. Three materials: matte charcoal graphite in
several values, deep petrol teal, and brushed copper-bronze with clearly visible
directional grain. Where planes meet, razor-thin brilliant hot edges catch a
single raking light. Faces turned from the light fall to near-black.

## What it teaches

**The whole round-two palette.** Spencer supplied it saying "this is what I think
of when I think of our color palette" and instructing that the round-one JPS work
be scrapped or modified.

It reframes the palette from a list of hexes into a material system: base value
plus surface behavior plus light response. It adds petrol teal as a co-primary
ground rather than an accent. And it disproves the round-one rule that gold must
be flat — brushed bronze with hot edges is machined, not plated, and reads
nothing like a law firm.

It also supplies the lighting model that unifies the brand: a single hard raking
light across faceted planes is **the same model as a folded paper crane**. The
mark and the environment are lit identically.

## Caution — the hexagons

The tessellation is the one part that must not transfer. Regular hex grids are
among the most exhausted motifs in circulation. Our geometry is origami folds:
irregular, asymmetric, unequal angles.

`plates/material-fold-relief.png` is this reference's materials rebuilt in fold
geometry — that is the working version.
