## What is in it

A vertical night scene. Enormous vertical banner-scrolls hang in a dark navy
void, each painted with landscape art — blossom, mountains, pines, calligraphy —
in cream, coral, teal and gold. They read as architectural columns but they are
unmistakably *paintings hung in space*, with no visible support.

Paper lanterns float at many depths and many sizes: some enormous and cropped by
the frame edge, some tiny and far. A small illuminated pagoda glows warm gold in
the middle distance. A single human figure in red stands on a shore at the
bottom, perhaps 2% of frame height. Below it all, mirror-still water reflects
everything.

## What it teaches

The clearest statement of both mechanics at once. Scrolls and lanterns are
objects whose real size the viewer knows, so their monumental treatment
registers as wrong rather than as scenery. The red figure is the ruler. The
mirror water doubles the composition. Depth is built entirely from stacked
planes separated by void.

## Caution

The palette is far more saturated and more Chinese-inflected than Pixel Blade —
vivid coral, teal, red on navy, plus red 東方 chop marks which are the source
