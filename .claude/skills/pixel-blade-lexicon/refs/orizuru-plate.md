# ref · orizuru-plate
**Tags:** `struck`, `material`

A single origami crane rendered dimensionally in white paper, sitting on
textured washi. Every facet is a flat plane with its own value; the creases are
visible as hard value breaks, not as drawn lines. A soft red disc behind it,
partly hidden by the bird. A hanko seal and a column of calligraphy.

**The mechanic.** *Facets, not outlines.* The crane reads as folded paper
because adjacent planes sit at different angles to one light and therefore land
at different values. Nothing is drawn — the fold is where two values meet.

**Why this one mattered.** The flat mark spent a whole round trying to imply a
fold with a single hairline in the page ground. Here the same fold reads
instantly and effortlessly because it is a real edge. That is the evidence that
the mark's problem was never its geometry — it was that a folded object was
being asked to live in a medium with no folds.

**Don't borrow:** the hanko, the calligraphy column, the literal white. This
brand's paper is washi-warm or petrol, and its seal is the mark itself.
