# brooding

Tagged references: `quietlab`, `focus-poster` (partial)
Brand-native plate: `plates/brooding.png`

## What Spencer means by it

The **emotional posture**. Restraint, weight, and no resolution. Patient rather
than sad. This is the register the brand speaks in when it is being serious, and
it is the tonal opposite of urgency.

Not: tragic, gothic, despairing, angsty, theatrical, or edgy.

## Mechanics

1. **One figure, alone, turned away.** Back to camera or in deep profile. No
   face, no expression, no gesture. `quietlab` sits its figure at a glowing
   screen in fog, entirely from behind.

2. **Larger than a `scale` figure, smaller than a portrait.** Roughly 15–25% of
   frame height. Big enough to carry feeling, too small to be a character. This
   is the distinction between `brooding` and `scale` — same device, different
   size, different job.

3. **No horizon and no destination.** The scene withholds resolution. Fog,
   mist, or blur removes the far distance. Nothing in frame indicates what
   happens next.

4. **Very low contrast.** Almost no highlights. The image sits in the lower
   third of the histogram and stays there. `quietlab` goes further and puts the
   whole photograph slightly out of focus — softness as emotional distance.

5. **Near-monochrome.** Colour drained close to grey with one faint warm note,
   usually distant and unreachable.

6. **Stillness.** No motion, no action, no blur from movement. Something has
   stopped, and is being held.

## Borrow / don't borrow

**Borrow:** the posture, the withheld resolution, the low contrast, the
turned-away figure.

**Do not borrow:** `quietlab`'s mauve-brown grade, and do not let brooding drift
into *soft*. The Pixel Blade version stays sharp where it is lit — this is a
brand about precision, so the blur is atmospheric, not a lens effect applied to
the whole frame.

## Copy register that goes with it

`focus-poster` carries the tone in words as well: *"BE PATIENT. PROGRESS TAKES
TIME, BUT IT CHANGES EVERYTHING."* Short declaratives, present tense, no
exclamation, the last line carrying the turn. That is the brooding voice —
worth noting because tone of voice and art direction have to agree or the page
reads as a stock photo with copy on it.
