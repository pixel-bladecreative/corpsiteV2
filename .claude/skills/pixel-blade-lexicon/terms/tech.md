# tech

Tagged reference: `future-dioramas` (four images, 2026-08-25)
Brand-native plates: `plates/tech-a-diorama.png`, `plates/tech-b-exploded.png`,
`plates/tech-c-fold.png`
Tokens: `brand/tokens.css` — the DAY MODE block.

Spencer's brief, verbatim: *"elegance, engineering, a dream of what the future
could be, but separate from 'cyber'... work in the 90 degree angles and hard,
Frank Lloyd Wright and Tadao Ando lines that may continue too far and fade into
nothing."* And: *"what does the future look like when made into a diorama,
textured with anything from wood and steel to construction paper, and made
achingly beautiful in 3-D."*

Standing instruction on this term: **stay in the dream.**

## Mechanics

1. **Ninety degrees, and nothing else.** Long horizontal slabs stacked and
   offset. Cantilevers reaching into empty air with nothing beneath them. Deep
   overhangs. One narrow slot of light cut through the mass. No curves, no
   organic form, no parametric blobs.

2. **Lines that overshoot and fade.** The signature. Hairline construction and
   dimension lines run far past the object, most of the way across the frame,
   and **dissolve to nothing before reaching the edge**. Node dots and tick
   scales sit along them. They imply a drawing the frame is too small to hold.

3. **Diorama, not building.** It is a *model* — hand-built, small, held. This is
   `scale` inverted: instead of a tiny figure proving a vast object, honest
   handmade texture proves a small one.

4. **Material honesty.** Board-formed concrete as fine matte card. Wood veneer
   with real grain. Hairline brushed steel. **Construction paper** in rust and
   teal, cut clean with slightly fibrous edges, each layer casting a real shallow
   shadow. If the materials look rendered rather than made, it has failed.

5. **Weightless.** Floating in empty ground — no table, no floor, no room. A
   tabletop photograph kills it instantly; that was the failure mode in testing.

6. **One warm light, from inside.** Amber from window slots or between layers.
   The same single-source rule as `night scene` and `material`.

7. **Not cyber.** No neon, no holograms, no glitch, no chrome, no blue glow, no
   sci-fi vehicles, no crowds. The future here is **serene and built**, not
   dystopian and lit.

## The bridge to the mark

**Construction paper is origami.** `tech-c-fold` puts built planes and folded
planes in the same object, and they read as one thing.

Tech is not a separate world from the crane — it is **the crane's material at
architectural scale**. That is what keeps this term inside the brand instead of
bolted onto it.

## Day mode

These references are the reason the site can have a light ground. All four are on
paper white, which is what Spencer meant by *"moving effortlessly from day to
night"* — homepage dark, paradigm and implementation pages light.

**The two unifying colors were already in the system:** petrol and oxide/rust.
Nothing new had to be invented.

| Role | Night | Day | On paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| ground | sumi #0A0A0B | paper #F4F5F2 | — |
| body copy | washi #E8E3D6 | petrol-deep #13252C | 14.44:1 |
| secondary | washi-dim | graphite #3F3937 | 10.36:1 |
| **accent** | **bronze #E59E5D** | **oxide #5E321F** | **9.83:1** |
| larger accent | bronze-hi | rust #7B4933 | 6.75:1 |

**The switch mechanism is bronze's failure.** It measures **2.05:1** on paper —
the hard constraint found back in round one. On a light ground its role passes to
oxide, which was already in the lit-world gamut. The roles hold constant and only
the values swap, which means the two modes are the same system and a page can
cross-fade between them.

On paper, bronze / wood tan / coral become **image colors** — decorative only,
never text. That is the `lit world` rule, inverted.

## Borrow / don't borrow

**Borrow:** all of the mechanics, the paper ground, the overshooting lines.

**Do not borrow the hexagons** from the first reference — same ruling as
`material`. Our geometry is folds and ninety-degree slabs.

**Do not borrow the printed text fragments** from the collage reference as
decoration. Same rule as calligraphy in `surreal`: if type appears it is
load-bearing.

**Do not let the light ground go pure white or cream.** #F4F5F2 is warm-neutral
with a hair of green-grey. Pure white reads clinical; cream reads nostalgic.
