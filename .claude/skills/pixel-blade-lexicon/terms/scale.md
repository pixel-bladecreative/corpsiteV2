# scale

Tagged references: `lantern-scrolls`, `scroll-hall`
Brand-native plate: `plates/surreal-scale--crane.png` (demonstrates it)
Counter-example: `plates/surreal-scale--hall.png` (omits the figure — see below)

## What Spencer means by it

One specific device: **a human figure used as a ruler.**

The figure exists to be measured against, not to be looked at. It is what
converts an ambiguous large object into a provably impossible one. Remove it and
the image is merely atmospheric; the viewer has no way to know whether the
scroll is three metres tall or three hundred.

## Mechanics

1. **1–3% of frame height. Never more.** At 5% the figure becomes a subject and
   the image becomes a portrait with scenery. At 1–3% it stays an instrument.

2. **One figure, or a small cluster.** A crowd converts the image into an event.
   One person, or two or three walking, keeps it silent.

3. **Turned away or in profile.** No face, no eye contact, no expression. The
   moment a face is legible the viewer reads emotion instead of size.

4. **Placed low, and small against the largest object.** Bottom edge of the
   frame, at the foot of the thing being measured. Usually standing on the
   mirror ground so the reflection doubles them and reinforces how little space
   they occupy.

5. **Often the only saturated element.** In `lantern-scrolls` the figure is the
   single red note in a navy field. In Pixel Blade terms the equivalent is
   restraint, not color — the figure is the only *sharp* thing, or the only
   fully black silhouette, in a hazy field.

6. **The figure never interacts with the impossible object.** No reaching,
   pointing, or reacting. They are simply present beneath it.

## Borrow / don't borrow

**Borrow:** all of the above. This device transfers wholesale — it is
palette-independent, which is why it survives into the brand cleanly.

**Do not borrow:** a red-dressed figure as the color accent. That is
`lantern-scrolls`' solution to its own palette, not ours. Ours is a dark
silhouette.

## Execution note from the plates

`surreal-scale--crane.png` gets this right: the figure sits at roughly 2% of
frame height on the mirror water, dwarfed by the crane, back turned, unlit.

`surreal-scale--hall.png` **fails it** — the scroll corridor and the void read
correctly but no human ruler is present, so the scrolls could be any size. It is
a good `surreal` plate and a poor `scale` one. Worth regenerating with the
figure explicitly placed before it is used as a scale reference.

When prompting for this, state the percentage. "Tiny" is not a specification;
"no more than two percent of the frame height" is.
