# surreal

Tagged references: `lantern-scrolls`, `scroll-hall`
Brand-native plates: `plates/surreal-scale--crane.png`, `plates/surreal-scale--hall.png`

## What Spencer means by it

**Not** fantasy, not creatures, not melting clocks, not glitch, not psychedelia.

Dislocation. Ordinary, culturally specific objects — a hanging paper scroll, a
paper lantern, a folded crane — reproduced faithfully and then placed at a size
and in a space where they cannot possibly be. Nothing in the image is invented.
Everything in it is impossible.

The unease comes from **fidelity plus wrongness**: the object is rendered with
total realism, so the mind accepts it, and then the scale and the absent physics
refuse to resolve.

## Mechanics

1. **Intimate objects, monumental size.** Choose objects whose real-world scale
   the viewer already knows in their body — paper, lanterns, scrolls, folded
   forms. Domestic scale is the baseline the image then violates. A monumental
   object made monumental is just architecture; it isn't this.

2. **No support, no explanation.** Nothing hangs from anything. No cable, no
   armature, no ceiling, no anchor point. If the eye can trace how the object is
   held up, the effect collapses.

3. **Void, not background.** The space behind is unlit and unbounded — not a
   sky, not a wall, not a gradient. Black that reads as *absence*. Edges of the
   frame dissolve rather than terminate.

4. **Interior and exterior collapsed.** The space reads as a hall and as a
   landscape simultaneously, and the image refuses to settle the question.
   Weather indoors. Architecture outdoors.

5. **Stacked planes with nothing between them.** Depth is built from discrete
   flat layers, each hazier and lower in contrast than the one in front. The
   gaps between planes are empty. This is atmospheric perspective doing the
   work, not perspective lines.

6. **Mirror floor.** A perfect unbroken reflective ground doubles the
   composition downward and doubles the available light. It also removes the
   last cue about where the viewer is standing.

7. **Absolute stillness.** No motion blur, no wind, no gesture. Everything is
   arrested. The surreality is calm, never frantic.

## Borrow / don't borrow

**Borrow:** the suspension without support, the void, the plane-stacking, the
mirror ground, the collapsed interior/exterior, the stillness.

**Do not borrow:** the saturation. `lantern-scrolls` runs vivid coral, teal, and
red against navy. Pixel Blade does not. Take the composition, discard the
palette — the plate `surreal-scale--crane.png` is the proof it survives the
translation to bone / black / #C5A05F.

**Do not borrow:** decorative calligraphy or readable text floating in the
scene. In this brand, if kanji appears it is load-bearing, not wallpaper.

## Why this belongs to Pixel Blade

A hanging scroll is paper. A crane is paper. **The environment and the mark are
the same material** — one folded, one hung. That is not a stretch imposed on the
reference; it is the reason this reference fits a brand whose mark is an origami
crane and whose ground is black.

It is also the mechanical answer to the "layered 3-D feel of moving through an
environment" requirement: stacked planes in a void, with a mirror floor, is a
parallax rig that already has an art direction.
